# Secure By Design editorial style

- Write the brand as **Secure By Design**, with a capital B in By, as requested by the user. Use the same spelling in prose, page titles, bylines, accessible labels, and email messages.
- Keep established navigation names: Home, Community, Compliance Blog, Technical Blog, Videos, and Cyber Range.
- Keep Community tab names: Career Goals, Cybersecurity Resources, and Get Involved.
- Use title case for article titles and established series or section names, including Active Directory Foundations and Body of Evidence & Artifacts. Use sentence case for ordinary section headings, captions, form labels, and messages.
- Preserve proper names and acronyms: Active Directory, Group Policy, AD DS, AD CS, DNS, SYSVOL, GPO, OU, FSMO, NIST, RMF, JSIG, DoD, and SAP.
- In prose, use “Group Policy object” and “organizational unit.” Capitalize actual Windows interface labels and named objects, such as Computer Configuration and the Domain Controllers OU.
- Use lowercase for generic roles in prose, such as system owner, security control assessor, and authorizing official. Retain capitalization in formal artifact titles such as System Security Plan.
- Use “Email address” consistently as the email field label. Use “sign in” as a verb and “sign-in” as a noun or adjective; “signup” is a noun and “sign up” is a verb.
- Preserve spelling in quoted material, official source titles, code, URLs, filenames, and example identifiers.
- Edit article Markdown and source page files, then regenerate content. Do not edit generated article JSON directly.

## Brand voice

- Speak as Secure By Design: emphasize its purpose, resources, learning opportunities, and community instead of the founder or site maintainer. Avoid author-facing “I,” “my,” “me,” and personal update narration.
- Keep the voice relaxed, direct, and welcoming. Prefer reader-focused language; use “we” for the brand where natural without implying a large team or unsupported capabilities.
- Use the same brand voice in page copy, metadata, form guidance, confirmations, and resource emails. Reader-voiced consent and actions (such as “Send my career goals”) are appropriate.
- Preserve quoted examples and fictional characters in technical teaching scenarios; these are not the website’s author voice. Keep planned labs and videos clearly labeled as coming soon.
