Before touching the router, write the policy: default deny between VLANs, and an exception register where every permitted path names its source, destination, port, and reason.
In this lesson you willTranslate the Inside the Network VLAN plan into a default-deny segmentation policy.Draft the access control list (ACL) exception register from the documented service paths.Record the gateway addressing and routing decisions before any device is built.Work through the configurationList the eight VLANs with their subnets and purposes from the network plan.
Reserve .1 in each subnet as the gateway for this sample, and record that no route crosses the air-gapped boundary.Write the policy sentence first: traffic between VLANs is denied unless a register entry permits it.
Segmentation alone does not establish enforcement — the access control lists do.Draft the register's first entries from paths the built systems already need: every VLAN to the domain controllers for DNS, Kerberos, LDAP, SMB, RPC, and time (UDP 123), because domain members take their time from the domain controllers; SBD-DC01, the primary domain controller (PDC) emulator, to SBD-TIME01 — the only system that syncs from the reference clock; and the admin VLAN to the tiers it manages.
One row per path: source, destination, service and port, justification, owner.Mark the paths that do not exist yet (workstation pools, business services) as planned rows, and decide the change process: who may add a register entry, and what evidence each addition needs.Inspect your configurationRun the relevant checks in an elevated PowerShell session — or a shell, on the Linux servers — on the host identified in the lesson.
