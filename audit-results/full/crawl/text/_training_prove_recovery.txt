In this lesson you willName the recovery set: what must come back, in what order.Set the recovery point and time objectives per item.Decide where backups live and who can touch them.Work through the configurationWrite the recovery set in dependency order — the same order the path built it: the directory first (everything trusts it), then the CA's database and keys, Group Policy, the router configuration, the log archive, and the living records themselves (register, inventory, exception record).Set the two numbers per item: how much history may be lost (recovery point) and how long restoration may take (recovery time) — lab-honest values you will actually measure against in lesson 5, not aspirations.Decide backup placement: each server's protected backup volume is written only by the backup process and readable by the recovery role, and a custodian carries copies to the offline backup cabinet on media — the air-gapped network's version of an off-site copy.
There is no network path to the cabinet.Confirm the standing custody items: the DSRM passwords are sealed and findable, and the root CA's protected backup from the course 1.4 ceremony is accounted for — recovery depends on secrets that must exist before the bad day.Inspect your configurationRun the relevant checks in an elevated PowerShell session — or a shell, on the Linux servers — on the host identified in the lesson.
