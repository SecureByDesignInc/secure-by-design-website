In this lesson you willDefine the update rings and their order.Set the one-approved-source rule per software type.Register the course's service paths.Work through the configurationDefine three rings and their order: a pilot ring (the course 1.9 sample machines plus one member server), the broad ring (fleet and member servers), and the critical ring last (domain controllers and both VLAN 30 hosts) — updates earn their way up, never down.Write the software rule: Windows updates come only from SBD-WSUS01, Linux packages only from SBD-REPO01, protection definitions only through SBD-EPO01, and anything else arrives as a documented exception — one source per type is what makes 'unauthorized software' a checkable claim.Decide the air-gap feeding cadence: what content crosses on media, how often, hashed by whom — the course 1.3 transfer procedure, now on a schedule.Add the register rows: every Windows system to WSUS (8530/8531), domain controllers included; the domain-joined Linux servers (SBD-SPLUNK01 and SBD-NESSUS01) to REPO01 (HTTP, GPG-signed packages); every Windows system's agent to Trellix ePolicy Orchestrator (ePO) on SBD-EPO01 on its agent-server secure port, TCP 443, with the agent starting each connection; and the scanner's credentialed reach into each VLAN (SMB 445 and WMI to Windows, SSH 22 to Linux) — the one deliberate wide permit, owned and logged.Inspect your configurationRun the relevant checks in an elevated PowerShell session — or a shell, on the Linux servers — on the host identified in the lesson.
